# English access and original-language provenance

Policy: `english-access-1.0`.

The active analysis/review pool requires either publisher-declared English audio, or a successfully retrieved nonempty English caption/transcript track. Non-English and unknown-audio videos do not qualify from an English title or description alone. Existing English tracks are preferred; successful YouTube auto-translation into English also qualifies. A CC flag or a “translatable” flag alone is insufficient. Blocked, failed, empty and unavailable retrievals fail closed and retain their distinct status. No proxy, cookie or access-control workaround is used.

English access is checked after the reproducible random draw and before source classification, comment collection, analytics and review export. Ineligible drawn videos are excluded without replacement. The original sampling records remain an audit trail; their probabilities describe the original draw, not a representative English-access population. Country/language coverage can therefore decline. The first batch also retains earlier immutable AI labels, but excluded videos do not enter the active review pool or new analytics. This policy is a separately versioned, timestamped record in `pipeline_runs`, not a rewrite of the original draw.

`original_language` for video audio preserves the publisher's `defaultAudioLanguage`; when absent, a single automatic caption track may provide the source-language basis. Unknown stays unknown. Metadata-text language remains separate. English caption source, automatic generation, automatic translation, retrieval timestamp and timed English segments are retained privately. The review player requests English CC and the page shows the retrieved English transcript explicitly. Source-video AI labels still score the title and description, not the transcript or unseen speech.

Every retained comment keeps `text_original`, its detected original language and confidence. A separate cached translation record contains `text_english`, translator-detected language, method, model version, timestamp and `label_language=en`. Confidently English comments retain their original wording without a translation call. Other comments are faithfully machine-translated with the existing model and weekly cost ceiling. Translation failure/budget exhaustion cannot silently fall back to labeling untranslated text. AI comment labels consume the English text; multilingual embeddings and duplicate analysis still use original text. Propagated labels record their representative separately from each comment's own language.

Human cluster review requires English text for every displayed comment, with original text and language available alongside it. Review imports check source eligibility again and attach the policy version. Validation only counts reviews under the current policy. Language is a content attribute that may inform audience analysis; it does not establish an author's nationality, location, identity or intended audience.

Caption/translation sidecars share the existing private service-only access and 30-day source retention. Raw transcripts and comments are never part of GitHub Pages. No new API credential or paid service is required.

YouTube's official [caption listing](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/captions/list) and [download](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/captions/download) endpoints require authorization; the public caption reader is pinned to [youtube-transcript-api 1.2.4](https://github.com/jdepoix/youtube-transcript-api). Availability from a local computer can differ from a hosted runner. Unknown access is reported honestly, not treated as proof that captions do not exist.
