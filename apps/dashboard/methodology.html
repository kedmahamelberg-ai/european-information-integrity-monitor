MEASUREMENT & LIMITS / HYBRID-FRAMING-1.0.1

Framing, not attribution.

The monitor studies how existing YouTube videos discuss war, security threats and coercion; how they portray countries; and how visible comments respond. The previous sectarianism taxonomy has been retired from the active workflow. Its records and prior human judgments remain separate and unchanged.

01 · Evidence and sampling

Only frozen sampled videos with successfully retrieved English transcripts or subtitles enter the classification pool. English audio alone is insufficient. Successfully retrieved YouTube auto-translated captions count, with their provenance retained. The original audio language is preserved; language is not proof of audience location. Missing captions mean exclusion from this analytical pool, not a negative topic label. Caption availability introduces selection bias.

Videos are sampled within the existing country × channel-tier frame before labels are known. The minimum available comment pool is 2. Up to 100 top-level comments are retrieved and up to 30 retained by the existing visibility ranking. Counts may include replies in YouTube’s total while our retained sample contains top-level comments. This sample is not representative of every viewer or comment.

The AI reads saved captions and metadata, not video frames. Long transcripts may exceed the 48,000-character input cap; truncation is recorded. Original comments are retained privately alongside saved English translations. Raw discussions and transcripts are not included in this website export.

02 · War and hybrid-threat relevance

Each video receives related, not related, or unclear. Related covers armed conflict, military readiness and coercive security activity such as cyber operations, sabotage, information operations, economic coercion and political interference. Ordinary political criticism, natural-disaster protection, tourism and economic hardship alone do not qualify. Current, historical, hypothetical and mixed contexts are separated.

Topic inclusion does not establish propaganda: neutral reporting, historical explanation and persuasive advocacy can all concern sovereignty or defence. Discussing a cyberattack or allegation does not establish a coordinated hybrid operation or identify its perpetrator. The scope draws on NATO’s overview of hybrid threats and the European Centre of Excellence for Countering Hybrid Threats.

03 · Portrayed military and security roles

Label What is portrayed
🛡️ Defence readiness Training, preparation, deterrence, defence or resilience against hostile action.
⚔️ Force projection & coercion Displaying military power, making threats, coercing or conducting an attack.
🎯 Target of hostile action Being attacked, hacked, sabotaged, threatened or coerced.

Roles are entity-specific and multi-label. An actor can be targeted and respond with force in the same video. Preparation is not automatically aggression. Role statements require source quotations and attribution in the private evidence record. Historical entities are not silently equated with current states.

04 · Execution: how the message is delivered

We adapt the five executional categories in Dall’Olio and Vakratsas, The Impact of Advertising Creative Strategy on Advertising Elasticity (2022 online publication; Journal of Marketing 87(1), Table 5). Read the paper ↗

Category Security-content adaptation
Comparative Explicit comparisons of actors/capabilities, or superiority claims.
Endorsement Expert, official or eyewitness testimony used to lend credibility.
Entertainment Humor, dramatization or a constructed story, beyond routine event reporting.
Imagery/visual Striking imagery, charts, montage or visual effects; requires watching.
Mnemonic devices Distinctive slogans, repeated names, memorable characters or audio cues.

Multiple categories can occur together. They apply only to war/security-related videos. AI transcript coding distinguishes present, not observed in transcript, and not observable. Visual features are not inferred from speech. A human can record absence from the full video only after watching it. Denominators exclude not-observable cases.

The source study analyzed 2,251 consumer-product advertising creatives with trained human coders. This adaptation has not been validated for conflict videos. Its advertising-effect findings cannot be transferred to war communication. We do not calculate an effectiveness, propaganda or danger score.

05 · Comments: stance is separate from sentiment

Agreement is assessed against an explicit, attributed video proposition: supports, opposes, mixed, no position, unrelated, unclear, or no video stance. A neutral report may offer no stance to agree with. Sentiment is positive, negative, mixed, neutral or unclear, with its target recorded independently. “That attack is awful” can be negative sentiment while supporting a video’s condemnation. This separation follows established stance-detection research.

06 · Engagement snapshots

Views, likes and total comments come from the original timestamped YouTube video statistics. Missing values remain unavailable, never zero. Shares are not part of public video statistics; owner-authorized Analytics access would be needed. We report likes and comments per 1,000 views where both counts exist, and the video’s age at capture. Views are not unique viewers, and platform counting rules differ between video formats.

Engagement can be associated with execution style, but these observational data cannot establish causation. Comparisons should account for channel size, video age, duration/format, topic, language and posting time. A single snapshot cannot measure engagement growth.

07 · Human review and publication

Initial calibration requests 30 eligible videos, or the whole eligible pool when smaller. Later batches use 3% rounded up, minimum 5, capped at the available pool. The random selection is independent of AI labels. Execution labels are reviewed only for relevant videos; comments have optional individual reviews. Calibration completion alone does not prove reliability. A second independent human coder would be needed to estimate human inter-coder agreement.

The public site shows metadata and engagement immediately. Individual reviewed cases can display their corrected labels. Remaining AI labels are released only when every assigned random-audit video is reviewed and the batch is fully classified. They are explicitly marked AI-coded and sample-audited, not individually human-reviewed. Completing this audit is a publication checkpoint, not proof of accuracy. Review exports preserve explicit agree/correct decisions, evidence basis, model version and source hash. No human judgment is pre-confirmed by the AI.

Model policy follows the Observatory: gpt-5-nano, then gpt-5.6-luna from 10 December 2026 UTC. The shared estimated model-spend ceiling remains $5 per week. Classification checkpoints are immutable and restartable. Raw data follow the existing 30-day retention policy.
