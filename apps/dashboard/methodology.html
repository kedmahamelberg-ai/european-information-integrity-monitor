MEASUREMENT & LIMITS / HYBRID-FRAMING-1.3

Framing, not attribution.

The monitor studies how existing YouTube videos discuss war, security threats and coercion; how they portray countries; and how visible comments respond. The previous sectarianism taxonomy has been retired from the active workflow. Its records and prior human judgments remain separate and unchanged.

01 · Evidence and sampling

Only frozen sampled videos with successfully retrieved English transcripts or subtitles enter the classification pool. English audio alone is insufficient. Successfully retrieved YouTube auto-translated captions count, with their provenance retained. The original audio language is preserved; language is not proof of audience location. Missing captions mean exclusion from this analytical pool, not a negative topic label. Caption availability introduces selection bias.

Videos are sampled within the existing country × channel-tier frame before labels are known. The minimum available comment pool is 2. Up to 100 top-level comments are retrieved and up to 30 retained by the existing visibility ranking. Counts may include replies in YouTube’s total while our retained sample contains top-level comments. This sample is not representative of every viewer or comment.

The AI reads saved captions and metadata, not video frames. Long transcripts may exceed the 48,000-character input cap; truncation is recorded. Original comments are retained privately alongside saved English translations. Raw discussions and transcripts are not included in this website export.

02 · Topics and country identification

Related covers security, sovereignty and the essential resources and systems on which a country depends. It includes water quality and scarcity, food supply, energy dependence, critical resources and infrastructure, public health, climate risks and civil resilience, as well as military conflict and hybrid threats. A national warning about unsafe drinking water and finite reserves qualifies under Water even when no military actor is mentioned. Ordinary lifestyle or tourism content without a substantive collective resource, security or sovereignty issue remains outside scope.

Topics replace the previous security domains. Several topics may apply: water; energy; food security; critical minerals and resources; climate and environment; critical infrastructure; health and biosecurity; economic and financial security; technology and supply chains; cybersecurity; information and influence; political institutions; sovereignty, territory and borders; military and defence; war and armed conflict; nuclear, chemical and biological risks; terrorism and organised crime; migration and displacement; diplomacy and alliances; maritime security; space security; civil protection and resilience; other security topics.

Country is identified automatically from names in the title and description. Original spoken language is separate metadata: Russian-language content does not necessarily concern Russia. For example, the groundwater video explicitly names Moldova. This identifies the country discussed, not the audience’s location or speaker’s nationality.

Resource constraints can be strategically relevant without proving propaganda, a coordinated hostile operation or an intention to invade. Topic inclusion must not be interpreted as evidence of any such intent. Temporal context is no longer a classification or filter. Dates and historical actor names remain in the evidence, so portrayal counts should not be read as current threat estimates.

03 · Portrayed military and security roles

Label What is portrayed
🛡️ Defence readiness Training, preparation, deterrence, defence or resilience against hostile action.
⚔️ Force projection & coercion Displaying military power, making threats, coercing or conducting an attack.
🎯 Target of hostile action Being attacked, hacked, sabotaged, threatened or coerced.

Roles are entity-specific and multi-label. An actor can be targeted and respond with force in the same video. Preparation is not automatically aggression. Role statements require source quotations and attribution in the private evidence record. Historical entities are not silently equated with current states.

04 · Execution: how the message is delivered

We adapt the five executional categories in Dall’Olio and Vakratsas, The Impact of Advertising Creative Strategy on Advertising Elasticity (2022 online publication; Journal of Marketing 87(1), Table 5). Read the paper ↗

Category Security-content adaptation
Comparative Direct or clearly implied contrasts of actors, capabilities, institutions, rights or living conditions, or superiority claims.
Endorsement Expert, official or eyewitness testimony used to lend credibility.
Entertainment Humor, dramatization or a constructed story, beyond routine event reporting.
Imagery/visual Striking visual presentation or vivid descriptive narration that evokes a mental picture. Both belong to this single category. The explanation identifies its evidence; a transcript cannot establish camera shots, editing or other unseen visual features.
Mnemonic devices Distinctive slogans, repeated names, memorable characters or audio cues.

The original five execution categories are binary: 1 when present, 0 when absent. They are not intensity scales, and multiple categories can occur together. Our evidence statuses keep “not observed in transcript”, “not observable” and “not applicable” separate from a verified 0. An explicit No requires watching the full video. Imagery/visual includes descriptive narration within the same category as a study-specific adaptation; no sixth category or imagery subtype is used. Present / assessed charts show evidence of presence among assessable cases, not a psychological intensity score.

The source study analyzed 2,251 consumer-product advertising creatives with trained human coders. This adaptation has not been validated for conflict videos. Its advertising-effect findings cannot be transferred to war communication. We do not calculate an effectiveness, propaganda or danger score.

Comparative execution includes direct and clearly implied contrasts grounded in the video, including citizenship rights, institutions and living conditions. Extending the paper’s direct-comparison definition to clear implicit contrasts is a study-specific adaptation. A factual description without a contrasted alternative does not suffice. Prior human reviews informed calibration. The latest resource-security scope supersedes the earlier exclusion of the groundwater video; prior records remain immutable. These examples do not independently validate the revised model.

05 · Comments: stance is separate from sentiment

Stance is classified only for comments and always refers to the attributed main proposition of the video: supports, opposes, mixed, no position, unrelated or unclear. The reference identifies the speaker, main message, target and speaker sentiment. Comment focus distinguishes substantive message/target evaluation from speaker or production praise. Condemning a reported attack is not by itself disagreement with a factual report that the attack occurred. Negative sentiment toward a target can support a speaker's criticism of that target. Sentiment and its named object are coded separately. The distinction follows stance-detection research.

06 · Engagement snapshots

Views, likes and total comments come from timestamped YouTube video statistics. The latest retained snapshot is shown; refreshes preserve the original records. Missing values remain unavailable, never zero. Shares are not part of public video statistics; owner-authorized Analytics access would be needed. We report likes and comments per 1,000 views where both counts exist, and the video’s age at capture. Views are not unique viewers, and platform counting rules differ between video formats.

Engagement can be associated with execution style, but these observational data cannot establish causation. Comparisons should account for channel size, video age, duration/format, topic, language and posting time. A single snapshot cannot measure engagement growth.

07 · Human review and publication

Initial calibration requests 30 eligible videos, or the whole eligible pool when smaller. Later batches use 3% rounded up, minimum 5, capped at the available pool. The random selection is independent of AI labels. Execution labels are reviewed only for relevant videos; comments have optional individual reviews. Calibration completion alone does not prove reliability. A second independent human coder would be needed to estimate human inter-coder agreement.

The public site shows metadata and engagement immediately. Individual reviewed cases can display their corrected labels. Remaining AI labels are released only when every assigned random-audit video is reviewed and the batch is fully classified. They are explicitly marked AI-coded and sample-audited, not individually human-reviewed. Completing this audit is a publication checkpoint, not proof of accuracy. Review exports preserve explicit agree/correct decisions, evidence basis, model version and source hash. No human judgment is pre-confirmed by the AI.

Model policy follows the Observatory: gpt-5-nano, then gpt-5.6-luna from 10 December 2026 UTC. The shared estimated model-spend ceiling remains $5 per week. Classification checkpoints are immutable and restartable. Raw data follow the existing 30-day retention policy.

08 · Message reference and audiovisual evidence

The video reference identifies its attributed speaker, main proposition, target and the speaker’s expressed sentiment. Comments are coded for their focus, agreement with that main proposition, and sentiment toward a named object. Negative comments about a criticized target can align with the speaker. Praise for speaking skill, editing or music alone does not establish agreement with the substantive message.

National laws, political institutions and international trade agreements are included in the broad study scope. Inclusion does not establish propaganda or hostile activity. A named official presenting evidence may function as a knowledgeable source; this does not verify the argument.

Imagery/visual remains one category and is not automatically present for every video. Thumbnail aesthetics cannot establish a full narrative, and background music alone is not an identity-linked mnemonic. Transcript music cues and retained thumbnails are aids to human review; no automated audio analysis is claimed.

These observations describe the retained sample. A single calibration batch cannot establish rising political capital, population opinion or a Europe-wide trend. Development examples used to revise coding rules are not an independent accuracy test.
