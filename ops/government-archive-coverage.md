# Automatic government archive reconciliation

The existing 09:00 and 15:00 Europe/Amsterdam collection dispatches also perform archive reconciliation. The first successful collection each Amsterdam day traverses each active source back to 1 October 2026 (the US benchmark starts 4 October). Later collections that day revisit 14 days of source history. A failed source retains its previous cursor and is retried by the next scheduled collection. Eleven pending sources remain disabled.

The US benchmark now covers all dated records in the White House **News** archive: releases, briefings and statements, fact sheets, presidential actions, remarks, and research. It does not claim coverage of every US federal department. The Canada benchmark covers all six types in the bilingual federal news index: news releases, statements, speeches, readouts, backgrounders, and media advisories. Provincial announcements and a separate prime minister portal are outside this index. The China benchmark remains the government's English News archive. Benchmarks remain separate from the 40 European-region sources.

The 26 November source inventory found 109 White House News URLs absent since 4 October (including one within the previous Briefings and Statements scope), and 338 Canadian English entries excluded by the old release-only filter since 1 October. China had 389 of 389 dated English News URLs retained at that check. These are acquisition counts, not AI classifications or proof that an editorial audit passed.

Canada dates come from the original article. Its publisher-declared English/French links identify translations; a shared timestamp cannot remove a distinct communication. Source dates and translation aliases are cached against the feed's revision timestamp. Existing IDs, human corrections and source-bound reviews survive rediscovery. Explicit CMS identities also prevent changed Kazakhstan slugs, San Marino routes, Liechtenstein links and Swiss language paths from creating duplicate records. Different announcements are never merged by title or timestamp.

The broader source review also found that Liechtenstein's default list does not reach the historical start date. Its collector now follows the publisher's actual date-filter form in bounded weekly windows, splits any window still displaying “more results”, and checks explicit empty results. The filtered `/1/` and default `/0/` links share the same publisher article ID. A 26 November source check traversed 81 unique dated releases from 2 October through 26 November; it did not claim their editorial audit was complete.

Every discovered identity is saved before its source cursor advances. Atomic checkpoint writes preserve the complete private translation and reconciliation state. Only new or pending valid work enters classification, with the existing 42-minute full-pass budget. Semantic output failures enter review instead of being blindly repeated. Routine three-percent review sampling and the independent full-source item audit remain separate checks.

After a successful main government deployment and completion of its source audit, `government-backlog.yml` can dispatch another classify-only pass from that exact run's cache. It requires successful classification progress and remaining fresh work, rechecks active collection/audit/publication and Brief import jobs, and leaves a 150-minute margin before both regular Amsterdam collection times (including summer time), plus a ten-minute dispatch grace period after them. The margin covers collection, deployment and audit timeout ceilings. The continuation explicitly excludes records carrying errors, except source-reviewed summary repairs; changed repair evidence enters review before any model call. Human and source-reviewed records are protected. Empty queues, missing checkpoints, pending calibration, zero progress and failed upstream runs stop continuation. This uses GitHub's documented `workflow_dispatch` exception for `GITHUB_TOKEN`; it requires no new personal token or Codex job.

Each collection saves `government-source-coverage.json`, including the exact archive URLs and retained IDs, in its artifact. After Pages deployment, the `verify-coverage` job compares that proof with live Monitor data, rejects missing identities and duplicates, and saves its result. It maintains one GitHub issue for missing or unverified source coverage, updates it when the findings change, and closes it only after complete live verification. A transport failure is recorded as unverified coverage, never zero announcements. Classification holds are not counted as missing acquisition.

For a read-only runner test, dispatch `government-news.yml` with `discovery_only=true`, explicit source codes, start date and an exact UTC cutoff. This mode uses the saved checkpoint for identity comparison but does not call a model, write Supabase, save a new cache, or deploy. It saves `government-source-discovery.json` with source status and newly discovered URL metadata. Run the actual backfill only after that test and all existing collection/deployment jobs finish.

Brief continues to import qualified Monitor records mechanically through its existing schedule. Broader acquisition must not turn every announcement into an AI story: only source-supported, qualified Monitor AI records are eligible. No second Brief classification or source fetch is introduced.
